# 06 — PROGRESSION

## 1. Four progression dimensions
1. **XP** — accumulated experience.
2. **Internal level** — an implementation metric used for progression calculations and unlock gates; not a primary HUD element.
3. **Professional grade** — the public career identity.
4. **Reputation** — current perception of the artist.

## 2. Level philosophy
The internal level is intentionally open-ended and can exceed 500. The game must not design itself around a hard cap of 50.

## 3. Unlock philosophy
Major features are earned through progression. Unlock conditions may combine:
- internal level;
- professional grade;
- skills;
- reputation;
- quests;
- certifications;
- achievements;
- market access.

## 4. Anti-farming
Repeatedly performing the easiest action must become progressively less efficient as an XP strategy.

Suggested calculation inputs:
- base activity difficulty;
- career relevance;
- risk;
- novelty / repetition count;
- quality of outcome;
- player eligibility.

The system should apply diminishing returns to repetitive low-value actions.

## 5. Career decline
Decline does not erase historical progression or reset the player to an initial state. Current reputation, contracts, audience, cashflow and opportunities may fall.

## 6. Comeback
A comeback is a structured progression path that restores career performance through meaningful actions rather than a free reset.

## 7. Anti-farming mechanics
Anti-farming is implemented through several mechanisms rather than a single XP cap:

- **Diminishing returns:** repeating the same low-difficulty activity in a short period progressively reduces its XP efficiency.
- **Contextual relevance:** activities aligned with the player's current career goals, grade and active quests receive better progression value than unrelated repetitive actions.
- **Cooldowns:** selected high-value activities have cooldowns or preparation windows before their full reward can be earned again.
- **Outcome quality:** poor performances, failed attempts and low-effort repetition provide less XP than meaningful successful outcomes.
- **Risk and difficulty weighting:** high-risk, high-skill or career-defining actions can provide greater progression value.
- **Variety bonus:** rotating between meaningful activities can restore or improve progression efficiency.

The goal is not to prevent repetition completely. The goal is to make **intentional career progression** more efficient than mechanically farming the easiest action.
