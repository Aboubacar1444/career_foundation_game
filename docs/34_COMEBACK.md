# 34 — COMEBACK SYSTEM

## Purpose
A career decline is a change of current status, not a deletion of the player's history. The comeback system allows a respected but weakened artist to rebuild through meaningful actions and strategic decisions.

## 1. Core principle
The player does not return directly to the beginning of the game after a decline.

The system preserves:
- historical achievements;
- certifications already earned;
- completed quests;
- historical peak grade;
- career milestones;
- relationships and past decisions;
- released music and its history.

A comeback therefore means **rebuilding present-day relevance using accumulated history**, not receiving a free reset.

## 2. Decline signals
A decline is evaluated from a rolling set of recent indicators rather than a single bad event.

Typical signals include:
- sustained reduction in audience activity;
- repeated underperformance of releases;
- falling reputation;
- loss of important professional relationships;
- contract losses;
- financial deterioration;
- inactivity in the artist's main market;
- unresolved negative events.

A single flop should normally create a setback, not trigger a full decline state.

## 3. Career states
The system uses five practical states:

### Stable
Current performance is healthy. No comeback process is active.

### Warning
Recent performance has weakened. The player receives signals and opportunities to correct course.

### Declining
The player has experienced sustained deterioration. Some high-tier opportunities may become less frequent, while recovery opportunities become more relevant.

### Crisis
Multiple negative indicators are active. Major contracts, media access or audience reach may be reduced. The player is still not reset to beginner status.

### Recovery / Comeback
The player has begun a successful reconstruction path and is recovering relevance.

Thresholds are configured relative to the player's current professional grade and market rather than using one universal numeric threshold for every career.

## 4. Concrete entry thresholds
For the initial implementation, a player enters **Warning** when at least 2 of the following persist across a rolling evaluation window:

- reputation falls by at least 10 points;
- active monthly audience falls by at least 20%;
- two consecutive significant releases underperform their expected audience band;
- a major contract or recurring opportunity is lost;
- cashflow remains negative across two evaluation periods.

A player enters **Declining** when at least 2 Warning signals persist into the next evaluation period, or when a severe event creates an immediate career shock.

A player enters **Crisis** when 3 or more severe decline indicators are simultaneously active, or when the player fails a defined recovery objective sequence.

These values are **initial balancing parameters**, not permanent design constants.

## 5. Recovery stages
A comeback follows a structured sequence:

### Stage 1 — Stabilize
Goal: stop the deterioration.

Examples:
- maintain positive cashflow for a period;
- complete a successful release;
- repair one important relationship;
- satisfy an overdue obligation.

### Stage 2 — Reconnect
Goal: restore access to people and audiences.

Examples:
- complete targeted collaborations;
- perform smaller but successful events;
- reactivate selected media relationships;
- rebuild a dormant audience segment.

### Stage 3 — Reposition
Goal: change the player's current market perception.

Examples:
- change release strategy;
- explore an unlocked genre or market;
- rebrand visual identity;
- hire different staff;
- target a new audience.

### Stage 4 — Return
Goal: regain a higher professional position.

Examples:
- achieve a meaningful chart result;
- earn a new certification;
- complete a major quest chain;
- secure a significant contract or tour opportunity.

## 6. Reactivation of opportunities
Lost opportunities do not automatically return just because time passes.

They can be reactivated when the player satisfies conditions such as:
- reputation recovering above a configured threshold;
- audience growth returning to a healthy trend;
- successful completion of a comeback quest;
- repaired relationship with the relevant NPC or organization;
- improved financial stability;
- successful performance in a target market.

Some opportunities may return in a modified form rather than exactly as before.

## 7. Memory of past events
The comeback system uses career memory.

Past events can affect:
- NPC trust;
- media narratives;
- fan loyalty;
- future quest availability;
- sponsor willingness;
- contract terms;
- comeback difficulty;
- special narrative opportunities.

A scandal remembered by the world may make the comeback harder, while a historic hit or respected certification may make recovery easier.

The memory system should distinguish between **historical fact** and **current perception**: the event remains true, while its influence can fade, recover or be reframed over time.

## 8. No instant restoration
A comeback should restore opportunities progressively.

Example:

`Crisis → Recovery → Reconnected → Relevant Again → Previous Grade Range`

The player's historical maximum grade is never erased, but current access is earned again.

## 9. Comeback rewards
A successful comeback may grant:
- reputation recovery;
- increased resilience or professional credibility;
- special titles;
- unique quest chains;
- comeback certifications/trophies;
- renewed market access;
- temporary media interest.

A comeback should feel like an achievement in its own right, not a punishment screen followed by a reset.

## 10. Design rule
The system must make failure meaningful without making failure fatal.

A strong career can decline. A damaged reputation can persist. A player can recover. The interesting outcome is the story created by the sequence, not the permanent deletion of progress.
